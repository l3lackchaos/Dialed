# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Specialty-coffee home brewers and baristas dialing in pour-over recipes. They use
this one-handed on a phone, in the kitchen, mid-brew — wet hands, a timer running,
a scale beeping. Thai-first audience; many switch between Thai and English coffee
vocabulary. The job: log a bean, save a repeatable recipe, and record how each
brew tasted so the next cup is better. Shared sessions let several tasters score
the same brew.

## Product Purpose
A barista's notebook that survives real kitchen use. Success = a brewer can log a
brew in under 20 seconds with one thumb, and later see at a glance which beans and
recipes score best. It replaces scattered notes and Notion tables.

## Brand Personality
Considered, warm, precise — a premium leather-bound brew journal, not a SaaS
dashboard. Three words: deliberate, tactile, quiet. It should feel like good
espresso equipment: heavy, well-machined, no wasted motion.

## Anti-references
- Generic admin/SaaS dashboards (Material cards, blue accents, stat-tiles).
- The AI-slop coffee aesthetic: cream/parchment body backgrounds, left side-stripe
  cards, "FEATURE 01" tracked eyebrows over every section, glassmorphism panels.
- Cramped modal forms stacked with fields — the previous version's main failure.

## Design Principles
1. **One thumb, mid-brew.** Big targets, short forms, the next action always obvious.
2. **Bilingual by default.** Thai and English are first-class; nothing hard-codes English.
3. **Earned familiarity.** Standard affordances (full-screen forms, sliders, lists),
   so the tool disappears into the task.
4. **Legibility over elegance.** Real contrast on cream-on-espresso; no decorative
   muted gray for body text.
5. **The data is the decoration.** Tasting scores, ratios, and radars carry the
   visual interest — not borders, stripes, or glass.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.1 AA: body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1 on the dark espresso surface.
Full keyboard + visible focus. `prefers-reduced-motion` honored. Touch targets ≥44px.
Thai and Latin scripts both rendered in a font that supports them cleanly.
